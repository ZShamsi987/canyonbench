\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What this measures for deployment.}
The quantity a deployed system needs is not average accuracy but the fraction of
output that can be trusted, and at what cost. Reporting presence accuracy,
localization, and causal reliance jointly makes the failure mode that matters
visible: a model that is accurate and well-localized but weakly grounded will
degrade silently under distribution shift, because nothing in its output signals
that the answer was not driven by the pixels. The acuity threshold gives an
operator a concrete deployment envelope---the apparent feature width below which
a given model's positives stop being evidence---and the extinction band gives a
regime where any positive is known to be ungrounded without needing a label.
Self-reported evidence cells are the natural interface for that check, because
they are already produced, require no model internals, and can be verified by
re-querying. CAVE is the minimal version of that verification.

\section{Limitations}
Procedural projection removes manual registration error but does not make map
sources infallible. Source vintage, consensus-layer dependence, orthoimage
artifacts, limited feature classes, synthetic camera geometry, and intervention
distribution shift remain limitations. Exact masks establish where registered
features project; they do not imply that a human or model can resolve them, so
extinction is analyzed separately.

Causal conclusions are operator-dependent unless O1--O3 agree and V2/V6 pass.
CAVE trades reliability for abstention and repeated-call cost; it is not a
guarantee of safe autonomous action. The first release de-scopes the optional
real-flight temporal tier.

\paragraph{Scope.} The benchmark evaluates operationally defined mapped features
in three classes over a narrow, controlled geographic slice. It is a causal
instrument, not a breadth benchmark, and it does not measure planning, action, or
multi-step reasoning. Grounding measured here is a necessary condition for
reliable aerial autonomy, not a sufficient one.

\section{Conclusion}
Whether a model \emph{sees}, whether it can \emph{point}, and whether it
\emph{relies} on what it points at are three different questions, and only the
first two are routinely measured. CanyonBench-Trace measures all three on the
same views, with an optics-grounded case where the correct answer is knowable
without annotation.
